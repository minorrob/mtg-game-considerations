Friendly Bracket 3

How I like to play Commander, offered as general guidance for a friendly table.

A couple of hours with friends, decks that get to do their thing, a game that goes the distance, and nobody leaves annoyed.

The game I'm here for

Most of us have families, jobs, and not much free time. When we do get to sit down, I want the game itself to be the fun part: the complexity, the politics, the big swings. I want to see what everyone built. I want to play it out rather than scoop early. And I want to walk away wanting to play again.

Power level

Bracket 3. Up to three Game Changers, no early two-card infinites, extra turns rare and never chained, no mass land destruction. Everything else is fair game.

Official definitions: Commander Brackets and the October 2025 update.

Before we shuffle

Say what your deck does and roughly when it tends to win. If it has a combo, say so. If it has something that stings, say so. Thirty seconds, no deck lore.

During the game

  • Let the first few turns breathe. Save removal for the things that matter.
  • Telegraph your big turn. If the combo is in hand, hint at it a turn early.
  • Shortcut known loops. Show the pieces, say the result, ask for responses.
  • Hit the threat, not the friend.
  • Call out triggers, yours and everyone else's.
  • Play to the end. Concede when you're locked out, never in response to something.

Stings are welcome

A Cyclonic Rift at the right end step, a Settle the Wreckage on a lethal swing, a stolen commander, a Craterhoof out of nowhere. These are the stories we tell later. The test is simple: is the person it hit still having fun two turns on?

Two things that drain a game

  • Reading every card out loud through a long combo string. Nobody needs the oracle text on turn fifteen of a loop. Pieces, result, done.
  • Telling other people how to play or what to put in their deck. Pilot your own seat. Deck talk is for after the game, and only if they ask.

When it hits you

Board wiped, commander exiled, spell countered, attacked three turns in a row. Shrug and play on. If you want to know why, ask: "what's making me the threat?" is fine. Complaining isn't. Anything bigger waits until the game is over.

After

Good game, every game. If something felt out of bounds, raise it once, after, about the play and never the player. If the table agrees, it changes. If not, it drops.

Written for one table. Take what's useful.
