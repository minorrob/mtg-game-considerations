Taking the hit

The things that sting, and how a table handles them without anyone getting bent out of shape. Each one has the generous response, the everyday baseline, and the version to skip.

The only complaint that belongs at the table is a question. The only protest belongs after the game, as a Rule 0 note.

What makes a game drag or sour. The tier names describe how a table tends to react, not who anyone is. Most of us have done something on this list; the point is knowing where it sits.

The game we're aiming for

A couple of hours that feel like a game, not a chore or a tournament. Every deck gets to do its thing at least once. The complexity is the fun. Stings are allowed. Bad feelings are not.

Definitions

Power level
Strong Bracket 2 to 3. Up to three game changers per deck, no mass land destruction, no chained extra turns, no two-card infinite combos before the late game, tutors present but not the engine.
Fun combo
A line that takes setup, is visible on the board, can be answered, and wins or swings the game in a way people enjoy watching. Kiki loops, aristocrat drains, counters doubling, Voltron with equipment.
Play it out
Games go to the end unless the result is mathematically locked. Nobody scoops at 20 life with cards in hand.
Every deck gets its moment
Each player should get to cast their commander, resolve their engine, and attempt their win before the game is decided, barring a bad draw.
Sting
A single play that hurts and makes someone laugh. A Rift at an end step, a stolen creature, a timely Settle, a surprise Triumph. The test: is the person it hit still having fun two turns later?
Late game
Roughly turn seven onward at this table.

Shape of a typical game

Turns 1 to 4Ramp, commanders, nobody gets punished for developing.
Turns 5 to 8Engines online, interaction starts, politics live.
Turn 8 onWin attempts, answers, a sting or two, game ends on a visible line.

Do

  • Declare power honestly before shuffling: bracket, game changers, win conditions, anything that stings.
  • Let the first four turns breathe. Save removal for engines and win conditions.
  • Telegraph: announce combo pieces in hand or on board a turn ahead.
  • Shortcut known loops: show the pieces, state the loop, state the result, ask for responses.
  • Play to the end. Concede only when locked out, never in response to something.
  • Spread damage by threat, not by seat.
  • Announce every trigger, yours and everyone else's.
  • Keep the board legible: life tracked, counters readable, tapped means tapped.
  • If an answer can wait a turn, let the deck work first.
  • Honor deals exactly as spoken.
  • Allow a takeback before new information is revealed.
  • Keep stings to about one per player per game.
  • Proxies are welcome.
  • Good game, every game.

Off the table

Card lines the table agrees not to run. A deckbuilding rule, not a judgment of anyone's collection.

  • Mass land destruction (Armageddon, Jokulhaups, Ruination and the like).
  • Chained extra turns. A single Time Warp is fine.
  • Two-card infinites before turn seven. No Thassa's Oracle lines at all.
  • Hard stax (Stasis, Winter Orb, Smokestack). Single tax pieces are fine.
  • Mindslaver effects that take a player's turn.
  • Permanent commander theft (Gilded Drake). Temporary steal effects are fine.

Table manners

  • No sandbagging. A declared bracket is the real bracket.
  • No narrating every card of a known loop.
  • No piloting other people's turns. Deck talk happens after the game.
  • No grudge targeting. Last game is last game.
  • No spite scoops.
  • No phone through combat.
  • No mid-game fairness debates. Park it for after.
  • No table-captain threat calls. Name a threat once, let people decide.
  • No post-game "I would have won."

Stings we're fine with

Done with a grin, roughly once per player per game.

  • Overloaded Cyclonic Rift once, at an end step, when the game needs a reset.
  • Settle the Wreckage or Teferi's Protection on a lethal swing.
  • Triumph of the Hordes or Craterhoof as a finisher.
  • Insurrection, or a single Control Magic effect.
  • Blood Moon, only if announced before the game and the table says yes.
  • Mill as a secondary win condition, not the whole plan.
  • Rhystic Study, Smothering Tithe: one tax piece at a time.

Raising something

  • After the game, not during.
  • Once, as a Rule 0 note: "next time, can we treat X as off the table?"
  • About the play, not the player.
  • If the table agrees, it goes on this page. If not, it drops.
